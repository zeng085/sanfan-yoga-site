Yoga Mat Printing Methods: UV Print vs Laser Engraving vs Embossing

Short answer: use UV (digital) printing for full-colour, all-over artwork, laser engraving for a single-tone logo or alignment lines, and embossing or debossing for a logo pressed into the surface. SANFAN runs all three in-house, and printed PU mats can be ordered from 1 piece, while TPE starts at 100 pcs per design and colour.

The three methods side by side

MethodLookBest forHow it works

UV printing: when you need colour

UV curing uses ultraviolet light to start a photochemical reaction that hardens the ink. Wikipedia describes it as a low-temperature, high-speed and solventless process. Digital printing in general is the method used for small-run jobs, because the image goes straight from file to surface.

That is why our custom printed PU mat page lists printing from 1 piece. The mat sits on a flatbed printer and the design prints directly onto it.

A flatbed printer running a pink mat at the factory.

Laser engraving: clean single-tone marks

Laser engraving renders a design by physically cutting into the object to remove material. It uses no ink and no tool bit that touches the surface. In yoga mats we commonly use it for alignment lines and logos. Our laser station is enclosed with fume extraction.

The mark has no colour of its own: it shows as a contrast or relief in the mat surface. Choose it when you want a quiet, premium look instead of artwork.

Embossing and debossing: pressed-in logos

Embossing presses a raised or recessed pattern into a sheet. In our process, sheets are sliced to thickness, embossed and stocked before finishing, so the texture or logo is part of the mat surface. A logo can be added by printing, laser engraving or debossing.

A mat sheet passing through an embossing roller.

How to choose

  • Full-colour artwork or a one-off design: UV print.
  • Alignment lines or a minimal logo: laser engraving.
  • A tactile logo or surface texture on every mat: embossing or debossing.
  • A brand mark in one or two flat colours on straps: screen print.
  • Combine them: an embossed texture with a printed or engraved logo is possible; confirm the combination on a sample.

What to put in the brief

  • Artwork file (vector for logos) and the placement on the mat.
  • Mat material, thickness and size.
  • Full colour or number of colours.
  • Whether the mark must be tactile.
  • Test reports your market needs, such as Prop 65 for California.

FAQ

Which method gives a full-colour all-over design on a yoga mat?

UV (digital) printing. It prints the artwork directly onto the mat surface, so full-colour designs do not need a separate screen for each colour. At SANFAN, printed PU mats can be ordered from 1 piece.

Which method is best for a simple logo or alignment lines?

Laser engraving is commonly used for alignment lines and logos, and embossing or debossing suits a logo pressed into the surface. Both are single-tone marks, so choose them for a clean branded look rather than colour artwork.

Does laser engraving use ink?

No. Laser engraving renders the design by removing material, with no inks or contact tool bits, so there is no ink to fade. The mark is a relief in the surface, not a coloured print.

Is there a minimum order for each method?

Our confirmed minimums are by material: TPE 100 pcs per design and colour, PU or rubber bulk OEM 300 pcs, and printed PU from 1 piece. The minimum for a specific logo method is confirmed in the quote.

Are printed yoga mats tested for chemicals?

We hold a Prop 65 test report for a printed PU yoga mat and RoHS 2.0 test reports for our mat materials. Prop 65 is a California warning law administered by OEHHA, so buyers selling there should keep the test report on file.

How do I brief a factory on the print?

Send the artwork file, the mat material and size, where the design sits, and the number of colours or whether it is full colour. Ask for a sample, which we turn around in about 7 days; sample fee and shipping are confirmed in the quote.
