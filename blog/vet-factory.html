How to Vet a Chinese Yoga Mat Factory Before a Bulk Order

Before you commit a bulk order, confirm you are dealing with a real manufacturer — not a trading company reselling someone else's product. Here is how.

Factory vs trader

A factory owns production lines, tooling and QC. A trader sources from factories and resells — adding a margin and a layer of risk. Both can deliver, but a factory gives you direct pricing, faster sampling and real accountability.

Proof a real factory provides

  • Business license with a manufacturing scope
  • Facility photos and video — not just product shots
  • Third-party audit reports (BSCI, SGS, ISO 9001)
  • A live video-call factory tour on request
If a supplier refuses a live video tour or only sends stock photos, that is a red flag.

Red flags to avoid

  • No facility video, or "can't show the floor for confidentiality"
  • Vague answers about production process and capacity
  • Prices that swing wildly with quantity
  • No verifiable export track record

Questions to ask before paying a deposit

  • Do you own the production lines, or source and resell? A factory names its lines; a trader names a supplier.
  • Can I see a live production video, not just a brochure?
  • Which certifications are current — ISO 9001, BSCI, REACH test reports?
  • Will you share batch-traceability records after the order ships?

Factory audit checklist

A real audit covers foaming, cutting, printing and packing under one roof. Look for in-house tooling (faster sampling), a QC station at each stage, and stable material inventory (shorter lead times). SANFAN runs a 12,000㎡ vertically integrated site in Jinjiang, Fujian, with 5 production lines and ISO 9001 plus BSCI audits on file — and we welcome a video walk-through or on-site visit before you commit.

Signs of a vertically integrated maker

Vertical integration shows up as speed and consistency: one facility takes a spec from foaming to finished carton, so a change in colour or thickness does not bounce between sub-suppliers. It also means one accountable party for quality. When a problem appears, you talk to the maker, not a trader relaying a factory they barely control.

Why it matters for your brand

Your customer remembers the mat, not the supply chain — but the supply chain decides whether that mat arrives on time, on spec and compliant. Sourcing from a real, integrated manufacturer protects your launch date, your review score and your repeat orders. That is the difference a factory visit (or a hard question) can save you from.

Payment terms that protect both sides

A balanced order uses a deposit to lock material and a balance on sight of shipping documents or before container release. Be wary of any supplier demanding 100% upfront, and equally of one refusing any deposit — both signal thin trust. A real manufacturer is comfortable with 30% deposit / 70% against bill of lading, because they have the facility and inventory to start work without your full payment in hand.

FAQ

How do I tell a factory from a trading company?

Ask for the business licence and check that its registered scope includes manufacturing, not just trading. Then ask to see the production equipment under the company's own name, and request a live video walk-through of the foaming and finishing lines. A trader can usually show a showroom but hesitates when asked to point a camera at the foaming machine.

Should I pay for a third-party audit?

For a first bulk order, yes. An audit by SGS, TUV or Intertek costs far less than a failed shipment and verifies what a supplier presentation cannot. For repeat orders with a proven supplier, a documented self-audit plus batch test reports is often sufficient.

What documents should a real factory provide?

Business licence with manufacturing scope, ISO 9001 certificate, BSCI or equivalent social-compliance audit if you sell into the EU, current third-party material test reports for your market, and batch traceability records. A genuine factory produces these within a day or two because it already has them.

Is a video factory tour enough?

A live, unscripted video call is a strong signal and filters out most traders, but it does not replace documentation or an audit. Use video to shortlist, then rely on certificates, test reports and a trial order before committing to volume.

大货前如何验中国瑜伽垫工厂

在下大货单之前，先确认你对接的是真实制造工厂——而非倒卖他人产品的贸易商。方法如下。

工厂还是贸易商

工厂拥有产线、模具与质检；贸易商从工厂采购再转售——多一层加价与风险。两者都能交货，但工厂给你更直接的价格、更快的打样与真正的责任归属。

真实工厂会提供的证据

  • 含制造范围的营业执照
  • 工厂实景照片与视频——而非仅产品图
  • 第三方审核报告（BSCI、SGS、ISO 9001）
  • 可按需提供的实时视频验厂
若供应商拒绝实时视频验厂，或只发库存图，那就是红旗信号。

需要规避的红旗

  • 没有工厂视频，或"因保密不能拍车间"
  • 对生产工艺与产能含糊其辞
  • 价格随数量剧烈波动
  • 无可验证的出口记录

我们能提供的

付定金前该问的问题

  • 生产线是你自己的，还是采购倒卖？工厂报得出产线，贸易商只报得出供应商。
  • 能否看实时生产视频，而不只是画册？
  • 哪些认证是有效的——ISO 9001、BSCI、REACH 测试报告？
  • 订单出货后是否愿意提供批次追溯记录？

验厂清单

真正的验厂要覆盖同一屋顶下的发泡、裁切、印刷与包装。看厂内模具（打样更快）、每道工序的 QC 工位、稳定的原料库存（交期更短）。三梵在福建晋江有 12,000㎡ 垂直整合厂区，5 条产线，ISO 9001 与 BSCI 审计在档——下单前欢迎视频巡厂或实地拜访。

垂直整合制造厂的迹象

垂直整合体现在速度与圆一贯：一个厂区把规格从发泡做到成品箱，所以改个颜色或厚度不必在多家代工间来回。它也意味着质量只有一个责任方。出问题你找的是制造厂，不是夹在中间、对工厂几乎没掌控力的贸易商。

为什么这关系你的品牌

你的客户记住的是垫子，不是供应链——但供应链决定了那张垫子是否准时、达标、合规地到达。向真实、整合的制造厂采购，保的是你的上市日期、评分与返单。一趟验厂（或一个硬问题）能替你避开的，正是这些风险。

保护双方的付款条件

平衡订单用定金锁原料、尾款见提单或放柜前付。警惕任何要求 100% 预付的，也警惕拒收任何定金的——两者都意味信任稀薄。真实制造厂能接受 30% 定金 / 70% 见提单，因为他们有厂区和库存，无需你全款到手才开工。

FAQ

怎么分辨工厂和贸易公司？

索要营业执照，看经营范围是否包含「生产/制造」而非仅为贸易；再要求查看以该公司名义登记的生产设备，并视频实拍发泡线与后道线。贸易公司通常能展示样板间，但被要求把镜头对准发泡机时会犹豫。

第三方验厂该花钱做吗？

首次下大货建议做。SGS、TÜV 或 Intertek 的验厂费用远低于一次失败出货的损失，且能验证供应商介绍里验证不了的东西。对已验证过的供应商复购，书面自审加批次检测报告通常已足够。

真工厂应该提供哪些文件？

含生产范围的营业执照、ISO 9001 证书、销往欧盟所需的 BSCI 或等效社会责任审核、面向你市场的当期第三方材料检测报告，以及批次追溯记录。真工厂一两天内就能拿出来，因为这些是现成的。

视频看厂够用吗？

不剪辑的实时视频是强信号，能筛掉大部分贸易商，但替代不了文件与验厂。用视频做初筛，再靠证书、检测报告与试单确认，最后才下大货。
