Reformer Springs Explained: Resistance, Spring Sources and Replacement

Short answer: a reformer spring is a helical steel spring whose stiffness (spring rate) sets the resistance, and there is no universal colour code, so brands define their own. SANFAN builds reformers with four spring sources (Wagner, German piano wire, Korean Sangbo steel, domestic carbon steel), chosen per model and resistance profile. Specify springs by measured resistance, source and count.

How a reformer spring creates resistance

Within its elastic range, a coil spring pulls back with a force proportional to how far it is stretched. Britannica gives this as F = kx, where k is the spring constant: a higher k means a stiffer spring. Engineering references define spring rate as force change divided by deflection.

For a round-wire helical spring, the usual formula is k ≈ G·d⁴ / (8·D³·n), where d is wire diameter, D is mean coil diameter and n is the number of active coils. In plain terms, thicker wire makes a stiffer spring; a wider coil or more coils makes it softer.

A reformer carriage is attached to one or more springs on a spring bar, so the springs you hook on set how hard the user pushes.

Why colours do not travel between brands

There is no shared standard that says red equals one resistance and blue another. A studio that mixes springs from two makers can end up with a machine whose colours no longer mean what the instructors expect. For an OEM or private-label order, define your own colour set and write the resistance for each colour into the spec sheet.

The four spring sources we build with

Our 2026 catalogue lists German Wagner springs, German piano-wire springs, Korean Sangbo steel and domestic carbon steel. Replacement and care

  • Springs are consumable parts: plan spares in the order.
  • Replace a spring that is rusted, damaged, stretched out of shape or no longer feels like the rest of the set.
  • Replace in matched sets so both sides of the carriage feel the same.
  • Do not mix springs of unknown resistance on one machine.

We do not state a lifespan in cycles, because it depends on use and we have no confirmed figure to publish.

FAQ

Are reformer spring colours standard across brands?

No. There is no industry-wide colour code for reformer springs. Each maker sets its own colours and resistance values, so always specify springs by measured resistance, not by colour name.

What makes one reformer spring heavier than another?

Spring rate: the force needed per unit of stretch. For a helical spring it rises with thicker wire and fewer or smaller coils, so a heavier spring is stiffer. Engineering references describe this with Hooke's law, F = kx.

Which spring materials does SANFAN use on reformers?

Per the 2026 catalogue, springs are German Wagner springs, German piano-wire springs, Korean Sangbo steel or domestic carbon steel, set per model and resistance profile. Tell us which source your brand needs.

How do I specify reformer springs in an OEM order?

Write down the spring source, the number of springs per machine, the resistance of each colour in the unit you use, and the bar position settings. Then check the sample against that sheet.

When should reformer springs be replaced?

Replace a spring that has lost its original feel, stretched out of shape, shows rust or damage, or no longer matches its set. Springs are consumable parts; we do not publish a fixed lifespan, because it depends on use.
