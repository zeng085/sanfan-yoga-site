PVC vs TPE

Yoga mats: PVC vs TPE compared

The table above covers both materials. These are the five points that actually decide it, and the answer we would give you on a call.

PointPVCTPE
Plasticisers, and odour when the mat is unrolled Contains plasticisers unless you specify otherwise, and the smell fades but rarely disappears. Contains no plasticisers and no phthalates, and the mat has no smell when it is unrolled.

Short version: PVC is the cheaper and more abrasion-resistant material, and TPE is the one that contains no plasticisers. Both are dry-grip products, so neither of them is a hot-yoga answer — that is PU on rubber. The only reason to choose PVC in 2026 is a price point that leaves no room for TPE, and TPE is the only one of the two that can be recycled at end of life. We make both, so we quote whichever you ask for; and we will tell you when PVC is going to fail your buyer's procurement, because a refused shipment costs more than the material saving.

Material by material

What each material is actually like in the hand

Written from what our own production lines see, including the failure modes — because the failure modes are what decide your return rate.

TPE — the default

Eco TPE, single or double layer
Feel
Soft, slightly tacky, warm to the touch — most people's idea of what a yoga mat feels like.
Grip
Dry grip is excellent. Wet grip is fair: fine for general and hatha classes, not ideal for hot yoga.
Watch out
Density varies a lot between suppliers. A cheap TPE mat flattens within months — ask for the density, not just the material name.
OEM
Widths 61 / 66 / 80 / 90 cm, 4–12 mm, single or double layer, dual-colour, embossed logo, printed strap and retail box.

PU + natural rubber — the grip benchmark

Moisture-absorbing top on a rubber base
Feel
Matte or suede-feel top, firm and dense — reads as a premium product the moment it is unrolled.
Grip
Gets grippier as it gets wet. This is the only family that stays safe in a 38°C hot-yoga room.
Watch out
Heavier than TPE, so freight per unit is higher, and the rubber base needs to be natural rubber — some suppliers substitute reclaimed rubber and grip suffers.
OEM
1.5–5 mm, alignment lines, matte or suede finish, HD printing, laser-engraved logo, travel-mat builds at 1.5 mm.

Suede & cork — for brands that sell on looks

Print-first surfaces
Feel
Suede is soft and cloth-like; cork is warm, dry and slightly textured. Neither feels like a commodity mat.
Grip
Both grip better once slightly damp — the opposite behaviour to PVC, and the reason they suit vinyasa and hot classes.
Watch out
Suede needs washing before first use to activate grip; cork dislikes being left in direct sun. Tell your customers, or you will get returns.
OEM
Full-surface HD printing on suede; laser engraving and screen print on cork. Base can be rubber, TPE or a foldable build.

PVC & NBR — still the right answer sometimes

Cost-led and cushioning-led builds
Feel
PVC is firm and glassy in cheap grades; NBR is thick and spongy and sinks under a knee.
Grip
Both are dry-grip products. NBR in particular becomes slippery when wet — do not sell it into hot yoga.
Watch out
PVC plasticiser odour and non-recyclability now fail procurement at many retailers and most hotels. We will still quote it, but we will say so when it is the wrong call.
OEM
Both take embossing and dual-colour. NBR is limited to emboss only — do not plan a printed design on it.
Certifications & testing

Our TPE, PU rubber and suede rubber mats are RoHS 2.0 tested and our printed PU/rubber mat is California Prop 65 tested; the factory is ISO 9001:2015 certified and was amfori BSCI audited in 2025 (renewal in progress). FAQ

Material questions buyers actually ask

Which yoga mat material is best?
There is no single best — it depends on the class. TPE for general practice and eco positioning, PU + natural rubber for hot yoga and professional studios, suede or cork for branded and design-led collections, NBR when thick cushioning matters more than grip. Tell us the class type and the retail price band and we will recommend one.
Is TPE really more eco-friendly than PVC?
Yes, verifiably: TPE is closed-cell, free of phthalate plasticisers, and recyclable at end of life; PVC contains plasticisers, cannot be recycled in practice and is the reason some retailers refuse mats on sustainability grounds. TPE also has no material odour, which matters for hotels and for unboxing reviews.
How thick should a yoga mat be?
6 mm is the volume standard for general practice. 4–5 mm is the studio and travel preference (better balance, easier to carry). 8–12 mm is for comfort-led, rehab and floor-exercise use. Over 12 mm the mat becomes unstable in standing poses, which is why we do not recommend it for serious practice.
Can you send samples of several materials to compare?
Yes. Tell us the materials and thicknesses you are considering and we send physical samples in the same size, with your logo on them, in about 7 days. Buyers usually compare TPE against PU + rubber and decide in the hand rather than on a spec sheet — which is the right way to decide.
