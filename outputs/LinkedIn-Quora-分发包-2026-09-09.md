## 一、LinkedIn 长文版（英文）

> 规格：1200–1800 字符最佳；前 2 行是折叠线之前唯一可见的部分，必须自成一个钩子；段落间空行；结尾一句 CTA + 3 个 hashtag。

```text
Most first orders with a Chinese yoga mat factory go wrong before production
ever starts.

Not because the factory is dishonest. Because the buyer asked the wrong
questions in the wrong order.

I've been on the production side of this category for 12 years. Four things
buyers consistently get wrong:

1. "Are you the factory?" is not a rude question — it's the first one.

Roughly half the factories you'll find online are trading companies. Some are
excellent. But you should know which one you're paying. Ask for the business
licence and check that the registered scope says manufacturing, not just
wholesale. Then ask for a live video call from the floor at 10am China time,
same day. A real factory says yes in five seconds.

2. MOQ is a colour-change rule, not a policy.

500 pcs isn't a factory rule — it's roughly the shortest run that justifies one
custom colour, because the line has to be purged between colours. Accept a
stock colour and only laser-engrave your logo, and 100–200 pcs is usually
achievable. Custom Pantone plus a custom size is what pushes it to 500+.

If you offer to take stock colour and the number doesn't move, you're not
talking to the factory.

3. "6mm TPE" is not a specification.

Two mats can both measure 6mm and differ by 30% in material. Invisible in
photos, obvious in your return rate. Ask for density (g/cm³) plus finished gram
weight, and get the tolerance in writing: ±5mm on length, ±3mm on thickness.
Then weigh the sample. 200g lighter than the approved sample means you were
quietly downgraded.

4. Nobody quotes you the real timeline.

Sampling 7–12 days. Bulk production 25–35 days after deposit and sample
approval. Ocean freight another 25–40 days. That's 60–90 days from first email
to your warehouse — and if a supplier promises 20 days door-to-door, they're
shipping stock that already exists, not making your product.

The pattern behind all four: the buyer compared unit prices, when they should
have compared answers.

If you're currently quoting a mat program and want a second read on the numbers
you've been given, send me the spec — material, density, MOQ, Incoterm. I'll
tell you which ones don't add up.

#manufacturing #sourcing #privateLabel
```

## 二、Quora 回答版（英文）

> 目标问题（搜索后回答，不要自己提问）：
> 1. `How do I find a yoga mat manufacturer in China?`
> 2. `What is the minimum order quantity for custom yoga mats?`
> 3. `How do I know if an Alibaba supplier is a manufacturer or a trading company?`
> 4. `How long does it take to manufacture and ship yoga mats from China?`
>
> 规格：600–900 词；第一句直接回答问题（Quora 摘要就取第一句）；用小标题分段；**结尾只放身份，不放链接**（Quora 对外链敏感，链接放个人简介）。

```markdown
Short answer: find them on Alibaba or Made-in-China, shortlist three, then
verify all three with the same nine questions before you send a deposit. The
sourcing part is easy; the verification part is where people lose money.

**1. Confirm you're talking to a factory, not a trader.**
Ask for the business licence (营业执照). The registered scope should include
manufacturing, not just 批发零售 (wholesale and retail). Then ask for a live
video call from the production floor during China working hours, same day. Ask
them to point the camera at the foaming line and tell you their daily output.
A real factory answers instantly; a trader has to "check with the factory".

About half of the suppliers you'll meet are trading companies. Some are very
good at what they do — just know who you're paying.

**2. Understand what the MOQ actually is.**
MOQ is a colour-change rule, not a policy. For TPE mats, 500 pcs is roughly the
shortest run that justifies one custom colour, because the production line has
to be purged between colours. So:
- Stock colour + laser-engraved logo: 100–200 pcs is usually achievable.
- Custom Pantone + your own size: 500+.
- Full-colour heat-transfer print: 300+ before it's economical.

Always ask for all three numbers. If the MOQ doesn't move when you offer to
take a stock colour, you're not talking to the factory.

**3. Specify density and weight, not just thickness.**
"6mm TPE" is not a spec. Two mats can both be 6mm and differ by 30% in
material — you can't see it in a photo, you see it in your return rate. Ask for
density in g/cm³ and the finished gram weight, hold them to ±5mm on length and
±3mm on thickness, and weigh the production sample when it arrives.

**4. Test the pre-production sample properly.**
Budget 7–12 days for sampling. When it arrives: dry grip, then wet grip with a
spray bottle (this is where cheap TPE fails); seal it in a bag for 48 hours and
smell it — that's the smell your customer gets; unroll it for 24 hours and check
whether the ends lie flat (the number one complaint from studios); weigh it;
fold it once, hard, and look at the crease. Then sign one sample, keep it, and
leave one at the factory. It's your only real leverage later.

**5. Ask for certificates by market, and for your product specifically.**
EU needs REACH SVHC; add EN71-3 if it's sold to children. The US needs CA Prop
65. Any recycled-content claim needs GRS with a transaction certificate.
Testing is per material batch, not per factory forever — a report with no
product spec, no date, or a different company name proves nothing.

**6. Get the real timeline.**
7–12 days sampling + 25–35 days production after deposit and sample approval +
25–40 days ocean freight = 60–90 days from first email to your warehouse.
Anyone promising 20 days door-to-door is shipping existing stock.

**7. Compare landed cost, not unit price.**
Get EXW, FOB and DDP quoted side by side. A cheap EXW price is often the most
expensive one landed, once you add inland haulage, export clearance and a
forwarder who's never shipped mats. Ask for pieces per 40HQ and check it against
your own carton dimensions.

**8. Inspect before you pay the balance.**
AQL 2.5 / 4.0, third-party inspector at roughly US$200–300/day — worth it on
your first two orders. At minimum, three pieces pulled from the actual batch,
carton photos, and a print colour check against your Pantone.

**9. Payment: 30% deposit, 70% after inspection.**
Anyone demanding 100% upfront is either very small or a broker. And if you're
paying for tooling or an embossing plate, get ownership in writing on the PI.

Run all nine against three suppliers. The one that answers all nine with
numbers — not "yes, we can do that" — is the one to sample with.

---
I work on the manufacturing side of this category (TPE, natural rubber and PU
mats). Happy to sanity-check a spec in the comments.
```
