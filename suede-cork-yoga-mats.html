Suede and Cork Guide

Suede or cork: two surfaces, two stories

Suede microfibre and natural cork are the two surfaces behind almost every design-led mat on the market, and they behave differently. Suede feels dry and soft, takes a high-definition print with real depth of colour, and grips harder as it gets damp — which is why heated studios specify it. Cork feels firmer and more grounded underfoot and sells on the material story rather than on the artwork.

The surface is only half the specification, because either one bonds to either base. Suede over natural rubber is the premium studio build, and suede over TPE gives the same print quality at a lighter weight and a lower landed cost. Cork pairs with rubber for the firmest feel, and cork over TPE brings the weight down for folding and travel formats.

Prints are where this family earns its margin: gradients, botanical and geometric artwork, florals, and licensed or collaborative designs, reproduced across the full face. Foldable builds pack into a smaller box without the mat curling at the edges, which is what mail-order and travel customers ask for. Send the artwork and we will sample it — MOQ is 100 pcs, samples in about 7 days.

FAQ

Suede and cork mat questions buyers ask

Suede or cork — how do we choose?
Suede if the range is sold on artwork and colour, because it holds a print far better and gets grippier as it gets damp. Cork if the range is sold on material and sustainability, because the surface itself is the story. Many customers run both: cork as the anchor line and suede for the seasonal prints.
Why do these surfaces grip better when wet?
Microfibre suede absorbs moisture into the fibre and the surface tension rises, so the hand holds harder as the mat gets damp. Cork does the same through its own cellular structure. It is the reason both are specified for heated and dynamic classes.
Which base layers can we pair with them?
Natural rubber for the firmest, most grounded build, or TPE for a lighter mat at a lower landed cost. Both surfaces bond to both bases, which is where the combinations come from. Tell us the retail price band and we will recommend one instead of quoting everything.
Can the mat carry our own artwork?
Yes — that is what this family is for. Gradients, botanical, geometric and floral prints all reproduce on suede, across the full face. Send the file and we will confirm how it samples before you commit to a production run.
How should these mats be cared for?
Wipe with water and a pH-neutral cleaner, dry flat and away from direct sun, and never put suede or cork through a machine. Cork can be refreshed with a dilute vinegar wipe. Looked after that way, both surfaces hold up for years of studio use.
