FAQ

PU rubber yoga mat questions buyers ask

How is a PU rubber yoga mat built?
A natural rubber base bonded to a moisture-absorbing PU top layer. The rubber stops the mat sliding on the floor and provides the cushioning; the PU gives the dry, matte hand-feel and the grip. It is the construction behind most premium studio and hot-yoga mats.
Why choose PU rubber over TPE for hot yoga?
Because PU grips better once it is wet, which is exactly the problem in a heated class — TPE gets slippery. For general retail at a lower price point, TPE is usually the better value. Tell us the class type and the retail price band and we recommend one rather than quote both.
What thickness and size options are there?
Thickness options start at 1.5 mm for travel and ultra-thin studio mats, and go up to 5 mm with cushioning. The common formats are 183 × 68 cm, 185 × 68 cm and 181 × 66 cm, and size, shape and corners are customizable. Suede-feel, matte and print-ready surfaces are all available.
How do we look after a PU rubber mat?
Wipe it with water and a pH-neutral cleaner, dry it flat, and keep it out of a hot car. Heat and detergent are what break the PU layer down; practice does not. Looked after properly, expect two to three years of daily studio use.
What is the MOQ, and can you match our Pantone colour?
MOQ is 300 pcs per colour and design, samples in about 7 days, and production 25 to 35 days after sign-off. PU rubber needs a longer cure than TPE, so book the launch window accordingly. Pantone matching on the PU top layer, debossed or printed logos and custom packaging are all standard.
